1. The Google AI tool

On June 3, 2026, the Utah State Board of Education said schools could use Google Gemini for Education, an AI tool that answers questions and helps with writing.

The board says it is an option, not a requirement. Each school district decides.

Tooele County School District signed Google’s data contract on June 11, 2026. That contract says how Google may use student data. We have not found a Tooele school board vote on it.

Some things the contract allows:

  • Google can change what the contract covers by updating its own website.
  • Google can keep student data up to 180 days before deleting it. The earlier limit was 60 days.
  • Student data can be stored in any country where Google has facilities.

2. The school apps study

Researchers from BYU and Internet Safety Labs tested 100 apps used in Utah classrooms. They watched what each app actually sent out over the internet.

  • About half of the apps that had a privacy contract (44 of 85) collected data the contract did not allow.
  • 6 in 10 apps shared data with other companies.
  • About 1 in 3 shared data with advertisers.

A new law, H.B. 55, took effect July 1, 2026. Rep. Tiara Auxier is the primary sponsor. It lets schools audit app companies and end contracts with companies that break the privacy rules.

Why it matters

Parents usually cannot see these contracts. A school board vote in a public meeting is one way families find out what their children’s schools agreed to.

The state rule still lets an app company check itself instead of having an outside audit. That is worth asking about.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

Your local school boardHas our district signed on to the state’s Google Gemini agreement, and was it discussed or voted on in a public meeting? Which EdTech apps does our district use, and which have a signed data privacy agreement?
Utah State Board of Education, student data privacy teamWhich districts and charter schools have opted in to the statewide Google agreement, and is that agreement posted publicly? St. George News listed the team’s contact as privacy@schools.utah.gov.
Your state representative and senatorThe state rule (R277-487-7(5)) lets vendors meet the audit requirement with a self-assessment. Should a true outside audit be required?
