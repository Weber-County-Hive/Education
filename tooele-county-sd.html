Case file — Tooele County School District

The Gemini Deployment No One Voted On

New here? In June 2026, Utah's State Board of Education rolled out Google Gemini to roughly 680,000 K-12 students — without a vote from the elected 15-member Board, and without the safety review, parental notice, or audit terms state law requires. Tooele County School District signed the underlying Google contract on June 11.

This case file covers what the contract itself says, what state law required at the time, and what the district's own records do — and don't — show.

What happened

On June 3, 2026, State Superintendent Molly Hart and USBE AI Specialist Matt Winters announced Google Gemini for Education would be made available across Utah's public schools — without informing or getting a vote from the elected Board, and without a procurement process, privacy review, or parental-notification framework in place beforehand. Tooele County School District signed the Google Data Processing Agreement (DPA) governing the deployment eight days later, on June 11.

What this case file does and doesn't establish. USBE's June 3 announcement made Gemini available statewide, to all Utah K-12 students — but availability isn't adoption. Only Tooele's signed DPA has been obtained and confirmed here. Whether other districts have signed the same contract, a variant of it, or nothing at all is not yet known. Treat the findings on this page as specific to Tooele's own agreement unless another district's contract is separately confirmed.

What state law required

Three statutes bear on a rollout like this. Records USBE released in response to a records request show none of the required documentation existed for any of them:

  • §53G-7-228(2)(a) — requires an evidence-based safety review before instructional technology is deployed.
  • §53G-7-1402(2)(j) — requires written parental notification before generative AI use, including a link to the district's metadata dictionary.
  • §53E-9-309(2)(a) — requires specific audit and termination provisions in a district's contract with a third-party technology vendor.
Timing matters here. H.B. 273, the 2026 law creating the safety-review and parental-notice requirements above, did not take effect until July 1, 2026 — about a month after the June 3 announcement and three weeks after Tooele's DPA was signed. Whether the announcement itself broke a law that wasn't yet in effect is a different question from whether classroom use after July 1 complies with it. Both questions are live; neither is settled here.

What the contract itself does

The Tooele DPA's terms consistently favor Google over the district that signed it:

  • Covered services are defined by reference to a Google-controlled webpage — meaning Google can expand what's covered, including adding Gemini features, without a new contract or a board vote.
  • The same mechanism governs which subprocessors can access student data — Google can add companies by updating its own site.
  • The contract's data-deletion window was extended from 60 days to 180 in a Google-authored amendment.
  • The 72-hour breach-notification requirement was replaced with vaguer "promptly and without undue delay" language, and "Data Breach" was relabeled "Data Incident."
  • Google may charge for any audit of its own compliance, and may reject an auditor it deems "manifestly unsuitable" — a standard that functions as a veto over who audits it.
  • No geographic limit exists on where student data may be stored — the contract allows processing "in any country where Google or its Subprocessors maintain facilities."

Tooele's own board record

No agenda item or minutes entry naming Google, Gemini, or the DPA has been found around Tooele's June 9 or June 16, 2026 board meetings — the deployment may have gone through administratively rather than as a board agenda item.

Tooele's Policy 5031 (Personal Technology and Communication Devices), rewritten in 2026, separately added AI-glasses regulation. Its definition of "AI glasses" closely mirrors the definition later written into state code by H.B. 44 — consistent with district staff aligning the policy to state code, as the district's own stated rationale holds.

Questions worth asking

  • Have other Utah school districts signed this same Google DPA, a variant of it, or nothing at all? So far only Tooele's has been confirmed.
  • A Washington County board member described "the option of a state contract" districts can opt into — is that Tooele's own agreement functioning as the statewide template, or a separate master agreement that hasn't been obtained yet?
  • If your own district hasn't formally signed anything, are students there still using Gemini in the classroom under a teacher's personal Google login, a school-level pilot, or informal use that wouldn't show up in a board record at all?
  • Does your district's board have a public record of voting on, or even discussing, a Gemini/Google AI deployment? Tooele's doesn't appear to.
