Who Connects to Whom: Hope of Utah, Sistema Utah and the Book Lists

One state grant line, two nonprofits, a State School Board seat and a book-rating website. Each line on this page is tied to a public record. The records show people in common. They do not show anyone coordinating.

Posted Nov. 2, 2026 · Updated Nov. 2, 2026: added Jamie Renda pay and gifts, Ogden ties, and Renda on the record

Short version
  • One grant line, two networks: in the 2025–26 budget year, the Legislature paid direct grants through the Department of Cultural and Community Engagement’s Arts & Museums line to Hope of Utah ($150,000) and to Sistema Utah’s “Youth Arts Access for Utah’s Future” ($100,000) (budget motions, Mar. 5, 2026; Sistema request).
  • Who runs them: Hope of Utah’s directors are Ron Williams (chair), Jamie Renda and Al Jackson (vice chairs) (2025 IRS Form 990-EZ). Sistema Utah’s trustees are Carolyn Sharette, David Sharette and Catherine Findlay (its IRS Form 990-EZ and Utah corporation filing). Carolyn Sharette leads American Preparatory Schools, a charter school management company.
  • Filed as education, paid as arts: Hope of Utah’s funding request form was filed under “Public Education,” directed to the State School Board. The Legislature heard it in its Economic and Community Development budget committee on Feb. 7, 2025, and paid it from the Arts & Museums “Pass Through Grants” line (Hope request; state budget record H.A.R. 143).
  • Now every year: in 2026 Rep. Lisonbee asked again, and the Legislature gave Hope of Utah $187,000 a year, ongoing, starting July 2026 (state budget record H.A.R. 49; H.B. 3, 2026, Item 210).
  • People in common: State School Board Member Joseph Kerry is listed as Hope of Utah’s “Former Co-Founder,” and Carolyn Sharette gave to his campaigns in 2024, 2025 and 2026. Cari Bartholomew sits on Hope of Utah’s advisory board and got 2024 campaign money from both Sharette and Brooke Stephens. Rep. Karianne Lisonbee asked for Hope’s grant and gave to Kerry’s campaign.
  • The book link: Hope of Utah’s own affiliates page lists “Rate My Books” (ratedbooks.org), the book-rating site run by Brooke Stephens. Kerry voted for Board Member Emily Green’s library review addendum and co-moved her Aug. 4 memo. See How a Book Gets Pulled From Every Utah School.
  • Still unknown: who received Hope of Utah’s $85,659.37 in pass-through money. Its state report does not name them.

What the requests show: Hope of Utah’s was the only request of the five that asked for ongoing, every-year money: $250,000 a year, all for “personnel services.” It was filed as public education money and paid from the arts line. Sistema Utah asked for $250,000, with $225,000 of it for salaries. Its 2024 tax return shows $24,791 in revenue for the whole year, so the $100,000 award is about four times that. The state’s payment register shows $90,000 paid to Sistema Utah in the 2025–26 year. It also shows Salt Lake County paying Sistema Utah every year since 2018 ($1,500 in 2018, rising to $13,460 in 2025), and American Preparatory Academy paying El Sistema USA $500 a year in 2014–2018 (Transparent Utah).

Grant, payment and report records came to the Hive from a records request; a tip came in. The dates on payment forms are request and approval dates, not proof of when a check was cut.

Numbers that don’t line up: the spending report prints a total of $150,140.65, which is more than the $150,000 award, and its own lines add to $150,140.86. The final report says $135,000 was received to date and $150,000 was used. It says the money helped “20+ non-profits” and names none of them. Hope’s 2025 tax return lists $48,050 in grants to unnamed “partner organizations.”
What the contract says about passing money on: Section 18 bars any “subcontract or sublet… without the prior written approval of the State.” Hope’s plan says it gives small grants to other nonprofits. No written approval is in the records we have. That doesn’t mean none exists.

The “20+ non-profits”: Hope of Utah’s 19 listed affiliates

Hope of Utah’s final report to the state says, “We were able to help 20+ non-profits.” It names none. Its spending report lists $85,659.37 in pass-through money and names no recipients. Its 2025 tax return lists $48,050 in grants to “partner organizations” and names none. Asked who received the money, the state department said its records had already been provided.

Hope of Utah’s own website lists 19 affiliates, plus one marked “coming soon” (hopeofutah.org/affiliates, read Nov. 2, 2026). We searched every one in the Utah Division of Corporations business registry on Nov. 2, 2026.

What the state registry shows: Hope of Utah itself is the registered agent for six of its affiliates, all formed as Utah nonprofit corporations between June 2025 and February 2026, during the grant year. Its office, 2351 Grant Ave., Suite 102, Ogden, is their agent address. Jamie Renda is the registered agent for a seventh. Hope of Utah’s chair, Ron Williams, is listed as the incorporator and chairman of an eighth, under the name Ronald Williams. Hope of Utah’s funding request said it would help nonprofits with “nonprofit filings” and “government paperwork.”

The book link

Hope of Utah’s own affiliates page lists 19 groups. One is “Rate My Books,” linked to ratedbooks.org. Utah corporation records list Brooke Stephens of Layton as the president of RatedBooks.org, a nonprofit formed May 16, 2025. State auditors used a list from ratedbooks.org in their 2025 follow-up audit of removed books. Its role in the State School Board’s library review is laid out in How one book list moved.

The 2023 opinion piece signed by Renda’s group lists a “Brooke Stevens,” Utah Parents United. The RatedBooks president’s name is spelled Stephens in news reports and state records. Both 2024 committee minutes for H.B. 261 list “Brooke Stephens, Education Director, Utah Parents United,” the same group named in the 2023 piece, speaking next to Renda. Official records use both spellings: the Feb. 9, 2024 Senate Education minutes on H.B. 29 say “Brooke Stevens, citizen.” So the same group, the same first name and two spellings in state records point to one person, but no record states it outright. We print the 2023 name as it appeared.

The affiliates page shows a listing, not money. No record we have shows Hope of Utah paying RatedBooks or any other affiliate.

  • No record shows campaign gifts changed how anyone voted.
  • No record names who received Hope of Utah’s pass-through or personnel money.
  • No news story we found names Jamie Renda and Carolyn Sharette together.
