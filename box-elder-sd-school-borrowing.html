Case file — Box Elder School District

Three Ways to Borrow

Voters turned down a $220 million bond. A petition stopped a $140 million lease bond. The board then used certificates of participation. Here is the record, step by step.

Posted Oct. 18, 2026 · Updated Oct. 18, 2026

New to this? Read it in plain words About 800 words, with one simple chart.
Short version
  • Nov. 2024: voters turned down a $220 million general obligation bond. KSL’s preliminary results showed 68.94% against.
  • Aug. 2025: after a Truth in Taxation hearing, the board raised its property tax revenue 36.54%. Five members voted yes; Karen Cronin voted no.
  • Dec. 2025: the board, sitting as its Municipal Building Authority, approved up to $140 million in lease revenue bonds, 7-0.
  • Jan. 2026: a petition to require a vote on those bonds was delivered (7,306 signers on the state’s page). The district put the lease revenue bond on hold.
  • June 2026: the board approved up to $55 million in certificates of participation, a lease paid from money set aside each year. HJ News reports a 6-1 vote.
  • Aug. 2026: $52.69 million was sold. Bond Buyer reports Moody’s lowered the district’s rating from Aa2 to A1 and rated the certificates A3.

Three ways to borrow

A school district can borrow for buildings in more than one way. Each way has different rules for who gets a say.

Tap each way to borrow to see who approves it and what happened in Box Elder.

General obligation (GO) bond

How it works

Voters approve it at an election. It is repaid with a property tax levy.

In Box Elder

Box Elder’s $220 million GO bond was on the Nov. 5, 2024 ballot. KSL’s preliminary results showed 68.94% against.

Source

Sample ballot; KSL

Lease revenue bond

How it works

Issued through the district’s Municipal Building Authority. The board does not need an election, but the notice says if 20% of registered voters sign a petition within 30 days, a vote is required (Utah Code 17D-2-502, as the notice cites it).

In Box Elder

Box Elder approved up to $140 million on Dec. 10, 2025. A petition was delivered, and the district put the bond on hold.

Source

Notice of bonds; Minutes, Jan. 14

Certificates of participation (COPs)

How it works

Investors buy shares of lease payments the board makes. The board decides each year whether to set aside the money (“annually appropriated lease payments”).

Before and after
BeforeLease revenue bond: notice included a petition right
AfterCertificates: the June 10 handout we read has no petition or referendum language
In Box Elder

Box Elder approved up to $55 million on June 10, 2026 and sold $52.69 million in August. We have not found a law that says either way whether certificates can be petitioned; the documents we read simply do not mention it.

Source

June 10 handout; Bond Buyer

What the district did by the book

  • It posted a Truth in Taxation notice July 21, 2025 and held the public hearing Aug. 13, where 138 people signed up to comment (notice, minutes).
  • Its Dec. 2025 bond notice told voters about the 20% petition right (notice).
  • When the petition came in, it put the lease revenue bond on hold (minutes, Jan. 14, 2026).
  • It noticed a June 10, 2026 budget hearing (notice) and posts its minutes on the Utah Public Notice Website.
  • U.S. Bank is the trustee on the certificates, as HJ News reported (HJ News).

The district’s stated reasons, in its own words

“We will need more facilities than we have right now.” (Neil Stevens, HJ News)

“I don't think it's actually going to save us any money to wait.” (Julie Taylor, HJ News)

“We have drastically reduced only to the needs.” (Danielle Wright, HJ News)

“Box Elder School District has elected to put the students first” (Supt. Keith Mecham, to Bond Buyer)

The district also told Bond Buyer the certificates need no tax increase. In May, Stevens said paying cash would use up the roughly $28 million capital fund (minutes, May 13).

The objection on the record

“taxpayers will probably see it as bypassing their vote on the bond” (Karen Cronin, Aug. 13, 2025, minutes)

“Trust is the currency of leadership.” (Cronin, HJ News)

What Moody’s said

Bond Buyer reports Moody’s cited weaker governance and higher social risk, saying the plan went ahead “despite strong opposition by the community.” Moody’s also said the district’s finances “will remain strong.” (Bond Buyer, Aug. 17, 2026)

We have not read Moody’s own report, which is not free to the public. These words are as Bond Buyer reported them.

Not confirmed

  • A quote attributed online to a board member, about not caring about re-election, is not in the hearing minutes or any news coverage we found. It is not used on this page. The hearing video is linked from the minutes for anyone who wants to check it.
  • The 2024 vote share (68.94% against) is KSL’s preliminary count. The official county canvass has not been read.
  • The June 10, 2026 vote (6-1) comes from HJ News. The official minutes for that meeting have not been read. HJ News does not date its story; matching it to June 10 is our reading of the agenda.
  • We have not seen a record of the clerk certifying the petition signatures.
